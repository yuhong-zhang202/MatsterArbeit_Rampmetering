# Stage 6 safe actuator feasibility: existing S17 evidence only

Status: exploratory, one seed (17), no new simulation. Source file paths and SHA-256 hashes are in `feasibility_summary.json`; the independent prior V9 audit is `../stage6_standard_metering_execution_20261003_v1/alinea_s17_v9/S17_REVIEW.md`. V8 is retained as a known unsafe reference, not a safe service benchmark.

Definitions were fixed before calculation: a crossing is a unique R ID in one `controller_steps.csv` crossing bracket `[t,t+1)`; inter-crossing headway is the integer difference between consecutive bracket starts within each named window. Mean command is the arithmetic mean over all seconds in the window. Actual rate is crossings divided by window duration. A ready front is the logged true lead vehicle with stopline gap in `[0,1.1]` m and speed `<0.1` m/s, with receiver clearance at least the required distance. Repeated nominal due rows are *seconds*, not unique vehicles. `window_summary.csv` contains the complete window accounting. Input schemas and 4200 consecutive step times were checked; both metadata files contain 4050 unique IDs with M3000/R600/U300/X150, and crossing IDs are unique R IDs.

In V9 the storage queue is nonempty over `[637,4200)`. In evaluation `[1200,3000)`, mean command is 1070.866 vehicles/hour, with 282 actual crossings or 564 vehicles/hour. The 281 within-window headways are 5–8 s, median 6 s, interquartile 6–7 s. Among 1204 nominal due seconds, 282 were allowed, 908 lacked a ready front or receiver, and 14 failed the follower stop-distance guard. Receiver clearance was available for 988 of 1800 seconds; the front met the stopline and speed test for 296 seconds. At due seconds with receiver ready, 450 had front gap >1.1 m and 238 had the front at line but moving at least 0.1 m/s; 220 due seconds had both front and receiver unready. This shows a large implementability gap under the current guard, despite continuous demand.

The first storage queue appears at t=637 and first crossing bracket starts at t=652 (15 s). For all 555 consecutive V9 crossing pairs, the first subsequent front-and-receiver ready moment occurs 5/6/7/8 s after the prior crossing in 72/254/213/16 pairs. Of those, 517 are released at the first ready second and 38 one second later. The detailed distribution and definition are in `intercross_readiness.json`. Thus the current stop-and-restart readiness rule explains most observed spacing. This is an observed guard-dependent delay, not an estimate of the network's universal discharge capacity.

V8 crosses all 600 requested R vehicles; its evaluation rate is 570 vehicles/hour, and the clearance-window rate is 654 vehicles/hour. That earlier actuator produced ten emergency braking warnings, including eight red-light emergency stops, so its faster clearance periods do not establish a safe rate or justify copying its transitions.

For the next reviewed executor, the measurement contract needs to separate commanded rate, scheduled phase, each stopline crossing, queued demand, front readiness, receiving clearance, follower safety, credit dropped/deferred, and startup delay. The release gate must verify safe transitions and actual tracking under sustained demand before copying a policy to seeds 23 and 42. A single V9 seed cannot justify a numerical safe upper bound; the new executor's attainable range must be demonstrated prospectively without changing the matched traffic inputs.
