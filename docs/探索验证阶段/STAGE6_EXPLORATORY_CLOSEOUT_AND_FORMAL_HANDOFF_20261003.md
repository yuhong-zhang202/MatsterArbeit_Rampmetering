Each of the three OPEN/control pairs matched for its seed before metering starts at 600 s; 238 E1 lane windows per controlled run were independently reconciled. V15 released exactly **600/600** requested R vehicles per seed as single intended crossings, with zero red/multiple/wrong-front or unsafe interlock events. Every one of the six fixed `[1200,3000)` 300 s windows per seed had storage demand in all 300 seconds; maximum actual-versus-command error was **5.33%, 4.27%, 5.46%** for seeds17/23/42, below the prospectively reviewed 10% screen. The 900 veh/h cap is a controller setting, **not** a demonstrated constant 900 veh/h discharge capacity. Sources: the three gate reports above.

For evaluation-period M vehicle-second-weighted speed in mainline cells13–17, OPEN→V15 is **9.666→25.903**, **11.836→26.024**, **9.958→26.328 m/s**. Full requested-cohort M observed system time falls by **236715/101168/191291 s** across seeds17/23/42. R system time rises by **306457/342595/351022 s** and U by **90585/103447/106691 s**. These are three paired seed observations at one demand; changing cell sample counts are retained, and M's total includes the removal of OPEN source waiting in seeds17/42. For the tested V15 policy, total M+R+U+X system seconds **increase** by **160524/345003/266804 s**. The policy therefore demonstrates a costly trade-off, **not** a feasible sweet spot or total-network benefit. ## Limits that remain after closeout

One-second FCD and SUMO warnings support technical integrity but cannot prove the model free of all possible defects or the city-delay mechanism uniquely causal.
