## Question and diagnosed mismatch

Stage 6 needs a middle controller that protects the freeway without serious R/U cost. The fixed default-model 22G+3y+35r program failed that role in a valid matched A/B pair: by 1500 s it admitted 170 R to the merge versus 226 under A_OPEN, and R/U mean full-route residence rose 153.821/26.473 s. `ramp_storage` and `shared_approach` E2 jam-positive bins were 44/90 and 22/90 (A zero). The M core speed fell 26.964→26.572 m/s and M trip duration rose 0.266 s, so 22G has **no observed freeway protection** in this one pair. It also imposes stronger-side queue/urban cost, contradicting its intended operational role as a moderate condition. The earliest M differences include nonlocal stochastic coupling, so the negative M change is an observation, not a physically attributed harm. The independent scientific B review assigns `B_NOT_IDENTIFIED` and holds the original C12G start.

## One calculation, one candidate

The R demand is 240 vehicles scheduled over `[540,1500)`, i.e. 900 veh/h over 960 s. There are 16 complete 60-s cycles in this interval. At 22 s green per cycle, 170 R had reached the merge edge by 1500 s, about 10.625 per cycle or 0.483 per green-second **of the whole observed interval**; this is not a saturation-flow measurement. A_OPEN had 226 at the same cutoff, leaving a 56-vehicle admission gap. Using **only a rough proportional extrapolation from one observed service point**, green seconds aimed at 216 R by that cutoff are `22 × 216/170 = 27.95 s`. Fix **one** milder 60-s signal candidate at **28G+3y+29r**, program ID `B_REBALANCED28`, before seeing its outcome. The 216 target is an engineering design aim, approximately ten fewer R than A by 1500 s, not an acceptance threshold. Its realized admission may differ because signal arrivals, queue dynamics, and merge travel are nonlinear. We do not estimate a precise saturation flow or transfer an external numeric threshold to this network. The 204.49 m compiled `ramp_storage_0` and 238.8 m `shared_approach_0` E2 coverage, plus 22G's observed 27/19 maximum jammed vehicles, motivate reducing retained R; they do not prove a physical spillback front. The earlier 22G arm reduced R admission yet failed to protect M; 28G is **not predicted** to succeed. It is one falsifiable test of whether a milder intermediate release can produce a nonlinear middle response without the observed strong queue cost.

The literature supports sizing metering rates against arrival demand and available storage and avoiding upstream arterial spillback ([FHWA Ramp Management Handbook, Ch. 10](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/ramp_mgmt_handbook/manual/manual/10_1.htm), [Ch. 6](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/ramp_mgmt_handbook/manual/manual/6_1.htm)). Those sources **do not** prescribe 28 seconds or a valid queue threshold for this synthetic SUMO network. The 28G value is a transparent project-specific methodological judgment from the observed one-run service and a bounded aim to avoid the already observed strong cost. It is not a calibrated optimum or ALINEA setting.
