## Proposed ALINEA law and measurement contract

At each completed 30 s boundary `t=630,660,...,4170`, use the **previous** complete half-open interval `[t-30,t)` from the existing `p1_main_down_20_l0` and `_l1` E1 detectors (two downstream M lanes, `main_down` position 20 m). Require both readings finite in `[0,100]`; compute their arithmetic mean `o_t` in **percentage points**. At `t=630`, confirm by an E1 XML/TraCI alignment test that the returned interval really is `[600,630)`, not `[570,600)` or a partial interval. If the API alignment fails, implement an explicitly tested 30-reading average of `getLastStepOccupancy`; never silently shift the feedback window or drop one lane with `nanmean`. A zero occupancy with valid detector coverage is a value, not missing data.

Proposed recurrence, applying the newly calculated rate to the following interval:

`r_t = clip(r_(t-30) + K * (o_target - o_t), r_min, r_max)`.

Candidate **only for review**: `o_target=11.0 percentage points`, `K=70 veh/h per percentage point`, `r_min=300`, `r_max=1200`, `r_init=900 veh/h`; initial rate applies from `t=600` until the first complete feedback at 630. Store the **clipped** command as the next recurrence state (no hidden windup), and log raw and clipped values. If either E1 value or timestamp is invalid, stop and retain the run as a measurement/technical failure; no substitute target or post hoc gain change. The feedback structure follows the occupancy-rate recurrence shown in [FHWA-HRT-13-097, Figure 79](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/operations/13097/13097.pdf); that study reports `Kr=70 vehicles/h`, but its occupancy normalization is not established here, so it does **not** validate this project's proposed `70 veh/h per percentage point`. The [FHWA detector handbook §3](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/operations/ITS/06108/03.cfm) also warns that cited default gains/occupancies are illustrative and not transferable to every application. Here `K=70 veh/h/percentage point` would equal `7000 veh/h` per occupancy fraction; never substitute `0.11` for `11.0` without the corresponding unit conversion. FHWA's displayed `1.17 veh/min` gain and fraction-style occupancy notation do not by themselves validate this project's unit choice or target. The [1991 ALINEA bibliographic record](https://trid.trb.org/View/365587) was found, but its full paper was not read in this review; no paper-specific numerical or implementation claim is made.

The 11% target is an **exposed-data calibration candidate**, roughly near the R600 free-flow candidate's downstream mean occupancy and below the R900 severe cases in the existing three-seed summaries. It is **not** an independently estimated critical occupancy, and target occupancy must not itself define or prove M improvement. Before any release, data/scientific reviewers must inspect the detector-specific occupancy–flow–state relation over the already stored R0/600/750/900 runs and decide whether one target/gain pair is defensible. If not, revise this design **prospectively**, rather than scanning targets/K after seeing controlled outcomes. The 30 s period matches existing E1 aggregation but is not an empirically optimized update interval.

## Proposed rate-to-signal execution and its uncertainty

Use only `ramp_mid` link index 0 (`ramp_storage → ramp_accel`), leaving `urban_tls` untouched. Proposed first actuator **technical candidate**, not a validated service model: direct G/r online states, one 1 s green slot per rate-requested vehicle and at least 2 s red between green starts. Convert `r_t` to slots with a persistent fractional credit: once per 1 s step add `r_t/3600`; when credit reaches one and the red minimum permits, issue a slot and subtract one. Do not reset credit at 30 s rate updates, do not bank an unbounded number of slots, and consume a slot even if no vehicle is waiting. This avoids an empty-demand token burst. At 300–1200 veh/h the nominal spacing is 12–3 s; the one-second/2-second-red candidate can schedule that range, **but it has not been shown to discharge one vehicle per slot in this network**. Record any capped/dropped credit. Do not replace the controller output with a green-share percentage or round each 30 s block's vehicle count independently.

At the TraCI boundary, specify and test whether a command made before `simulationStep(t+1)` controls `[t,t+1)`. Set the first metered state at t=600; before then `ramp_mid` remains `A_OPEN`. Apply the same adapter through t=4200, including the demand-free clearance period. **Do not open the meter at t=3000** to hide queued R cost. If there is unfinished R/U/M/X demand at 4200, retain the censored cohort and scheduled-to-observation-end time; do not compare only completed trips. Actual `ramp_mid` crossing, first `merge_section` appearance, auxiliary-to-M lane change and downstream passage are separate events.

This one-second direct G/r pulse is a falsifiable implementation proposal, not a physical-safety or service-capacity claim. A future bounded technical test must detect zero-, one- and multiple-vehicle green slots; red crossings; startup loss; yellow-transition or emergency-braking effects; and actual service under sustained queue. If one vehicle per slot, safe transition and effective rate tracking fail, mark the actuator **not verified** and return to prospective engineering/scientific amendment. Do not quietly switch pulse width, yellow policy, `r_min/r_max`, or target after seeing whether M improves. The [FHWA detector handbook §3](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/operations/ITS/06108/03.cfm) discusses rate-to-signal timing and its implementation constraints. This proposed pulse must be described as an unverified SUMO actuator for the ALINEA-style feedback law, not as a verified reproduction of a field implementation.

At the candidate 300 veh/h lower bound throughout `[600,3000)`, nominally 600 requested R would face only 200 scheduled slots, leaving up to 400 before accounting for startup loss or source limitation. Even 1200 veh/h throughout the 1200 s clearance period offers only 400 nominal slots. This arithmetic makes the 4200 s censoring and city queue record essential; it does not predict the actual queue.

## Release gates and smallest possible run sequence

1. **Design gate:** independent data review of 11%/70 units, detector curve and cohort-cost contract; independent scientific review of feedback question, exposed seed use, actuator candidate, censoring and stop rules. The current document alone is not a release. No new `EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL` status or decision promotion is inferred.
2. **Static/software gate:** new runner and pure logic tests validate feedback sign/units, clipping without windup, `[t-30,t)` timing, fractional pulse scheduling across updates, no empty-credit burst, min red, one controlled link, and unchanged non-ramp inputs. A card must bind new source/runtime, SUMO, network, all three input files, parent OPEN card/receipt, controller parameters and output paths. The old OPEN runner remains immutable.
3. **No-intervention neutrality gate:** before relying on existing OPEN output, one newly registered TraCI **all-green no-op** technical run at S17 with byte-identical demand and otherwise identical config must match the stored S17 OPEN trajectory/lifecycle/E1/TLS record after normalizing run-specific headers and paths; check 4200 labels, every vehicle tuple and aggregate outputs, not just exit code. If it differs, diagnose the runner; if deterministic neutrality cannot be established, new same-runner OPEN comparators for each seed are required before policy inference. A single S17 pass plus static seed-independent code review is the **minimum** basis to reuse S23/S42, with residual seed-specific neutrality risk stated; reviewers may require the other two no-op checks.
4. **Actuator gate:** one newly registered S17 controlled technical/exploratory pilot under the locked candidate. Require complete command/state/vehicle-crossing trace; command state aligns with actual SUMO TLS state each second; no red crossing or more than one vehicle per intended green slot; no unreported collision/teleport/emergency-braking or missing detector interval; requested-rate, slot and measured-discharge time series reconcile. If actual service is biased or pulse transitions are unsafe, stop before S23/S42 and record a technical failure. One pilot does not become formal confirmation after the fact.
5. **Comparison gate:** only after the above pass and a separate exact-card execution release, compare S17 OPEN/control, then run S23 and S42 sequentially under **the same locked policy**. Retain negative or mixed results. Report M P/L/S and spatiotemporal speed/density, complete M requested-cohort time and downstream completions; R requested/inserted/at-signal/through-signal/merge/lane-change counts and queue at 3000/4200; U/X internal/source/shared-connector costs; all-class system time, censored counts, and clearance-period residuals. Analyze paired differences by seed (n=3); do not treat seconds/vehicles as independent replications or claim a precise success probability.

The plan's global ceiling is **40 total starts**, with **18 already consumed**, and a separate cap of **8 conditional control/technical starts**; these counters must span old and new runners. The minimum sequence is one no-op S17 + three controlled seeds = **4 new starts**, leaving four of the conditional eight for documented technical contingencies, not automatic retries or parameter sweeps. If no-op equivalence requires all three seeds, the sequence is **6 starts**. Every failure consumes a start. Preserve 120 s wall and 250 MB on-disk per run, and the shared 8 GB raw ceiling (existing archived raw including receipts: 197,935,338 bytes at the Oct 2 stop). Before each start, verify actual current counters, size, release hash, one-use reservation and available budget; do not reset caps in a new runner. No exact card or run is prepared by this design.
